Workout A

About 45 minutes. Rest 90 seconds between sets. Tap any name for a form video.

    Workout B

    Dumbbells, pull-up bar, and a chair for your back foot.

      The part that actually builds muscle. The last two reps of every set should be hard — not impossible, but close. When you finish the top of the rep range with room to spare, go up in weight next session.

      Working up to a pull-up. Jump or step up so your chin clears the bar, then lower as slowly as you can. Lowering builds the strength to pull long before you can pull.

      Eating day

      Pick whichever option you want each day. The calendar remembers what you chose.

      MAIN MEAL · 7–9 AM
      10–12 oz steak or 8 oz fish, with a generous serving of mashed potatoes or mac and cheese. Scaled up, since this is now one of only two feedings. ≈ 1,100–1,250 cal · 70–80 g protein
      VEGETABLES
      Spinach, kale, broccoli, salad, carrots or peppers. One serving, and make it leafy green or deep orange — those carry the most of what this diet is short on.
      PROTEIN SNACK · MID-AFTERNOON
      Two cups of cottage cheese or Greek yogurt, not one. With nuts or trail mix stirred in. ≈ 500 cal · 50 g protein
      FRUIT
      Berries, citrus, or an apple. The easiest way to cover more vitamins in one go — C, folate, potassium and antioxidants all arrive together.
      TOP UP AS NEEDED
      Extra veg or beans, trail mix, nuts, or extra protein. With only two feedings, use this slot more often than you'd think — and beans are the single most useful thing on it, covering fiber, folate and magnesium at once.
      WATER
      Tap once for each 8 oz glass through the day. Add chia to a glass when you want it — it brings fiber and omega-3s, but it counts here as hydration first.

      ≈ 1,700–1,800 cal · ≈ 120 g protein — if the portions scale up

      Two feedings changes the portion math. Dropping the evening meal removes roughly 400 calories and 30 g of protein. If the remaining two stay the same size, you land near 1,200 calories, which is under what you burn — and muscle doesn't get built in a deficit. The portions above are scaled to compensate.

      Watch the protein ceiling. Your body can only use so much protein per sitting for building; the rest gets burned for fuel. Two large feedings work, but they're less efficient than three moderate ones. If your strength numbers stall for a month, this is the first thing I'd revisit.

      Fish at least twice a week. It's your only real source of omega-3s and it offsets the saturated fat that daily ribeye piles up.

      But keep the red meat. Steak is your best iron source and iron matters more for you than for most people. Four steak days and three fish days is the balance to aim for.

      Worth testing rather than guessing. Vitamin D is the one nutrient here you can't reason your way to. Ask for a blood level at your next checkup.

      Weekly weigh-in

      Same morning each week, before eating. Half a pound a week is the target.

        Lift log

        Log your hardest set. The score blends weight and reps, so more reps at the same dumbbell still counts as progress.

          Backup

          Progress is rarely a straight line. A flat week is normal; three flat weeks is a signal to eat a little more. General fitness guidance, not medical advice.
